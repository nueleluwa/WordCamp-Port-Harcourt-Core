# WordCamp Central Compliance

**Purpose:** The rules WCPHC must follow because it operates under WordCamp Central and the WordPress Foundation, not because the local team chose them. These are not negotiable at the local level — if a decision anywhere in this documentation seems to conflict with one of these, the rule below wins and the document should be corrected.

**Reference:** Official handbook — https://make.wordpress.org/community/handbook/wordcamp-organizer/. Every organizer should read it directly at least once; this page is a working summary, not a replacement.

## The five core rules

### 1. Not-for-profit
All WordCamps must be not-for-profit events. See [planning/budget-and-finance-principles.md](../planning/budget-and-finance-principles.md) — the financial target is break-even, not surplus maximization.

### 2. Open to all
Events must be open to all participants, regardless of experience level. This shapes ticket pricing (free/subsidized student tickets — see [reporting-and-kpis/kpi-framework.md](../reporting-and-kpis/kpi-framework.md) registration breakdown) and program content (sessions spanning beginner to advanced).

### 3. Speaker slots are never for sale
Speaker slots cannot be purchased or influenced by sponsorship. No sponsorship tier or package includes speaking time, and a talk is never offered, implied, or guaranteed in exchange for sponsoring — not in outreach emails, sponsorship decks, agreements, or conversations.

A sponsor's representative can still speak, but only separately from the sponsorship: either by applying through the Call for Speakers, or by being invited by the Program team to speak on a specific topic. Either way, the decision is made on the merits of the topic and the speaker, goes through the same review as every other submission ([speakers-and-program/call-for-speakers-and-program-guide.md](../speakers-and-program/call-for-speakers-and-program-guide.md)), and is never tied to what the sponsor paid.

### 4. All organizers are volunteers
No organizing team member is paid for their role. See [00-start-here/welcome-and-philosophy.md](../00-start-here/welcome-and-philosophy.md) "volunteer-driven" principle and [organisation/accountability-and-escalation.md](../organisation/accountability-and-escalation.md) for how workload is managed without pay as a lever.

### 5. Financial transparency
Required, not optional. See [planning/budget-and-finance-principles.md](../planning/budget-and-finance-principles.md) for what this means in practice (budget vs. actual tracking, post-event financial report, archived records).

## What compliance looks like day to day

- **Sponsorship team:** never offer, imply, or accept a request for speaking time as part of a sponsorship, in any tier or negotiation. If a sponsor wants to speak, redirect them to the CFP process, or hand the topic to the Program team as a separate matter.
- **Program team:** review every submission — including sponsor-affiliated ones — against the same criteria.
- **Finance:** keep the budget vs. actual comparison current throughout planning, not reconstructed after the fact.
- **Anyone:** if you're unsure whether something complies, ask the Lead Organizer before committing to it externally (in an email, a signed agreement, a public promise) — it's much harder to walk back a compliance issue after a sponsor or speaker has been told something.
